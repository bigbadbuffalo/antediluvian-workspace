## 2B: Aramaic Book of Giants

The Qumran **Book of Giants** survives in multiple fragmented Aramaic manuscripts, including 4Q203 and 4Q530–533; the separately transmitted **Manichaean Book of Giants** was adapted in later Iranian/Old Turkic languages. James R. Davila's 2025 introduction provides an annotated scholarly reconstruction, but its A/M episode numbering represents modern alignment of scattered fragments, not a single intact ancient manuscript. [Publisher-accessible chapter excerpt](https://bibleinterp.arizona.edu/sites/bibleinterp.arizona.edu/files/2025-04/Davila-BauckhamExcerptGiants_0.pdf), SRC-027.

**Claims 069–075:** The text mentions named giant figures, including **Ohyah, Hahyah, Mahaway and Gilgamesh**; Hahyah dreams of a watered garden and destructive fire/water, while Ohyah relates a vision of judgment. The giants seek **Enoch as an interpreter**. The genuine appearance of Gilgamesh is an ancient Jewish encounter with a Mesopotamian legendary figure. It **does not** prove direct copying from the received Gilgamesh epic, that the heroic epic's whole plot occurred, or that all ancient gods/giants were one race.

The text is fragmentary; some names, dream order and links to Manichaean fragments are editor-reconstructed. Manuscript archive [4Q203](https://www.deadseascrolls.org.il/explore-the-archive/manuscript/4Q203-1?locale=en_US), [4Q531](https://www.deadseascrolls.org.il/explore-the-archive/manuscript/4Q531-1?locale=en_US). **Not yet done:** full Stuckenbruck/Puech editions, 4Q530 verse-by-verse textual apparatus, separate Manichaean originals.

## 2C: Book of Jubilees

**Selected checked English text:** [Jubilees 4–5, 7, 10](https://adfonteslibrary.org/work-st-pseudepigrapha-jubilees) (SRC-030). The book is normally placed in the Second Temple period, with fragmentary **Hebrew** copies at Qumran and a complete later **Ethiopic** text. Its exact literary growth and extent in earlier witnesses remain questions; [Monger 2018](https://doi.org/10.2143/RQ.30.2.3285631) explores variant transmission, but only the article abstract was accessible in this pass.

| Passage | Primary-source finding | Source distinction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Jub. 4:9–10 | Cain's wife is named Awan; Cain's city is called after Enoch | Genesis 4:17 lacks his wife's name |
| Jub. 4:15 | Watchers originally descend to **instruct human beings in righteousness and judgment** | This differs importantly in narrative framing from a simple “all descended solely to corrupt humanity” account |
| Jub. 4:17–23 | Enoch writes, tracks months/seasons and testifies against the fallen Watchers | Does not define literacy in the entire antediluvian population |
| Jub. 5:1–11 | Angel-human unions, giant offspring, their corruption and punishment | Compare Enochic witnesses separately before harmonizing |
| Jub. 10:1–14 | Demons afflict Noah's descendants, a tenth remain under Mastema, Noah receives healing instruction | **Explicitly post-Flood** in its narrative; not a direct pre-Flood medical or demonic bureaucracy |

**Claims 076–080.** The chronology's dates and week/jubilee structure belong to the *text*, not a universally verified world chronology. Need critical Hebrew/Ge'ez comparison and specialist editorial notes.

## 2D: Genesis Apocryphon (1Q20)

[Posen Library's translated excerpts](https://www.posenlibrary.com/entry/conception-noah) of **1Q20 column II** (SRC-032) attest Lamech's anxiety that Noah was conceived by a Watcher; the same story gives his wife **Batenosh** a rebuttal affirming Lamech's paternity. This ancient text is *not* proof that Noah had angelic ancestry or that antediluvian humans used biological engineering. Its full translation is access-restricted, so only the displayed lines and the institution's interpretive summary were checked.

**Claims 081–083.** Keep **Noah's father Lamech** distinct from Cain's descendant Lamech (Genesis 4); do not automatically unite the two genealogies. Need the complete 1Q20 witness, detailed manuscript dating and related “Birth of Noah” fragments 4Q534–536.

## Key cross-source contrasts, without fictional resolutions

1. Genesis names Cainite human craft ancestors; 1 Enoch names angelic teachers of dangerous arts. No ancient-source priority determination by simple blending.
2. Jubilees 4:15 offers a Watchers' originally righteous mission, while 1 Enoch 6–8 emphasizes transgression and corrupting instruction.
3. The Book of Giants offers **giants speaking, debating, fearing, dreaming and seeking interpretation**—not merely a nameless class of monsters. This is a feature of the attested story, not yet a fictional character design.
4. The Genesis Apocryphon presents **contested family testimony** in the voice of human characters; it does not assert an unambiguous divine/giant parentage.
5. Jubilees 10 locates Mastema's surviving spirit narrative *after* the Flood; use source chronology before importing any motif into a pre-Flood era.
