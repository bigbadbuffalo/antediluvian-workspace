# Pass 3 — Jubilees

**Checked 2026-11-10.** Chapters 3–10 in James C. VanderKam's 2018 Hermeneia translation as enhanced by the Jubilees Palimpsest Project (SRC-042), compared selectively to Charles (SRC-030), the Hebrew inventory and the Latin witness history. The date of the work, dates within the story, and dates of surviving witnesses are separate.

## Witness and composition controls

Jubilees frames the primeval history as revealed to Moses on Sinai (1:1–4, 26–29). It is an ancient Jewish rewriting of Genesis and the opening of Exodus, originally in Hebrew. A mid-second-century BCE composition is a working estimate. Machiela (SRC-041 pp. 37–41) reports VanderKam's historically argued 161–140 BCE window, with a narrower preference of 161–152 BCE, alongside earlier proposals such as around 168 BCE. The argument involves historical allusions and proposed knowledge of Enochic material. It cannot independently date every section or literary layer. Monger's 4Q216 study (SRC-031) is a control on composition and manuscript variation. The oldest relevant Cave 4 dates remain palaeographic estimates.

### 4:15 — descent and the original mandate

Charles's rendering describes instruction in judgment and uprightness. VanderKam's 2018 wording coordinates teaching humanity with doing what is just and upright on earth. That grammatical distinction matters for who is expected to do the just acts and whether those acts are specified as the direct content of instruction. In either translation the scene assigns a positive purpose to the descent. The same work distinguishes the later breach at 4:22, the angels whom God had sent at 5:6, and Noah's retrospective accusation that they exceeded their authorized remit at 7:21. Compare 1 Enoch 6:1–8, where the oath and taking women motivate the narrated descent, with 7–8 and 16:3 on illicit instruction. Watchers has no parallel statement there of an authorized initial mission. The works narrate different openings, and an attempt to sequence them as a single event would go beyond either account. No Hebrew copy of the mission phrase was verified and the Latin leaves are lost. The next reading is the Geʿez 4:15 text and VanderKam's textual note d.

### Literary relationship and next work

Jubilees 5:1–11 shares angelic unions, offspring killing one another and fathers witnessing the loss with 1 Enoch 6–10, in a retelling that also preserves the Genesis 6 judgment and 120 years. Jubilees 4:17–26 and 8:3 know Enochic subjects including writing, heavenly order and illicit teachings, but give distinct causes, emphases and chronology. For the chapter 8–9 geography compare the arguments for direction of dependence and for shared material in Machiela (SRC-041 pp. 27–41), alongside the actual passages. Do not infer direct dependence in every case.
