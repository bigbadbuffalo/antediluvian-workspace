**Dating.** The Jewish Book of Giants is a Second Temple Aramaic composition. A composition in the Hellenistic period, often estimated around the third to second centuries BCE, is a working range. The recovered copies have individual palaeographic estimates, broadly late Hellenistic into Herodian times. Copying date and earlier composition are separate. 1Q24 and 4Q533 have weaker identifications than manuscripts with distinctive names or direct overlaps. Individual dates for every scrap were not re-derived. [Marro, §§3.2, 5.1–10](https://edoc.ub.uni-muenchen.de/36601/1/Marro_Inaki.pdf) · [Machiela, ch. 2](https://brill.com/display/book/9789004513815/BP000012.pdf).

## 2. Passage checks and reconstruction disputes

- **Names and relationships.** 4Q203 4:3 and 7a:5, 4Q530 2 ii and 6Q8 1 support distinct speaking roles for Ohyah, Hahyah and Mahaway. Mahaway's attribution of fatherhood to Baraq'el has support at 6Q8 1:4 and the 1Q23 29 parallel. Parts of Ohyah and Hahyah's kinship depend on restoring the dream column or comparing subsequent witnesses. The Iranian recensions also supply paternal and fraternal links. The comparatively clear name Hobabish in 4Q203 3:3 resembles Humbaba/Ḫuwawa. Marro's identification of an adjacent broken sequence with Akka of Kish is a conjecture, and Ahiram at 4Q531 4:1 has its own editorial controls. Do not convert neighboring names into a complete family tree.
- **4Q203 8:3–15.** The documentary opening has a second tablet/letter, Enoch as scribe, and an addressee read as Shemihazah and companions. The accusation involves unions, offspring and earth's complaint. Later lines are used to reconstruct a warning, bond imagery and a call to pray. The change from letter framing to particular reconstructed speech must remain visible. Set alongside the Watchers petition in 1 Enoch 13–16, this gives Enoch both a scribal and revelatory role without equating the two letters or their outcomes. See Marro pp. 210–215 and Stuckenbruck DJD XXXVI.
- **4Q530 2 ii and joins.** In Marro's extended alignment (pp. 288–298), Hahyah's dream includes a garden, water and fire/destruction, with lines about the surviving tree/shoots substantially supplied. Ohyah's second vision in that arrangement includes a throne scene, books opened and a declaration of judgment. Some words in the reconstruction come from small joined or aligned pieces 8–11 and elsewhere. 6Q8 2 brings roots and a garden but does not independently supply the longer narrative. Stuckenbruck's 1997 sequencing paper uses Middle Persian M 101 j to test the ordering of the tablet and garden dream fragments. Puech argues a closer connection to 4Q530, with details disputed. Consequently the order of the dreams in one modern reconstruction and the distribution of dream imagery among manuscripts require separate citations. Compare Marro §5.6 and §5.10 and Stuckenbruck (SRC-040).
- **4Q530 7 ii.** The reconstructed journey has Mahaway traveling to Enoch in a remote landscape, with the dream interpretation requested for the giants. Some language of flight or movement is fragmentary. A fuller episode about wings and danger from the sun appears in the Old Uyghur witness, below. Earlier speech/“before” and the second tablet make two missions possible, but the number and order of all missions are reconstructive.
- **Violence, boasting and impending loss.** The Watchers foregrounds the sufferers and celestial response. Giants fragments also give the offspring direct speech, exchanges of fear, disputed interpretations, boasting about power and efforts to obtain information. 4Q531 22:4–12 contains damaged strength/conflict and dream language. Speaker and immediate opponent should be attributed by edition. 4Q532's partial food shortage and corruption passage and 4Q203's earthly complaint preserve the human and ecological costs alongside the giants' anxiety.

### Gilgamesh

4Q530 2 ii:2 in Stuckenbruck's 1997 locator (compare the extended joins and line numbering in Marro §5.6) and 4Q531 22d:12 (Puech, Marro §5.7, pp. 398–401; 17:12 in an earlier sequence) are the control points for Gilgamesh. Marro prints two related spellings, with damage and editorial placement differing between the contexts. 4Q531 22 includes Ohyah's speech and a dream. The difficult 4Q530 opening involves Gilgamesh/Hobabish material in reconstruction, with the extent of physical preservation and additional identifications open. This confirms ancient Jewish engagement with a Mesopotamian figure. It leaves open the vehicle of transmission and the use of any particular text or epic episode.

Goff (2009) argues for creative appropriation, including a reading of defeat and curse, while Marro (§4.2.1 pp. 97–101) disputes how much about defeat or cursing can be assigned to Gilgamesh. Mesopotamian traditions also make Gilgamesh an underworld figure beyond the narrative of the Standard Babylonian Epic. The roles of judge of the dead, dream interlocutor, combatant, and recipient of a curse require separate evidence. Enkidu's role in the Cedar Forest episode does not license an identification in the Qumran scraps. [Goff, DSD 16](https://doi.org/10.1163/156851709X395740) · [Marro, chs. 4–5](https://edoc.ub.uni-muenchen.de/36601/1/Marro_Inaki.pdf).

## 3. Mani's Giants and later transmission

Mani was a third-century CE religious founder. The Turfan witnesses are later physical copies and translations, chiefly in Middle Persian, Sogdian and Old Uyghur. Dates of individual scraps and the direction of every translation relationship require catalogue work. Coptic Manichaean discussions of the material have a different evidentiary status from an actual fragment of Mani's book. Use Henning's editions with Morano's update, Marro's appendix and Wilkens for the Old Uyghur evidence. [Henning 1943](https://sacred-texts.com/chr/giants/giants.htm) · [Morano](https://rep.adw-goe.de/bitstream/11858/00-001S-0000-002C-DD18-F/1/9783110261370AdW17_07_ENRICO%20MORANO%20%20New%20Research%20on%20Manis%20Book%20of%20Giants.pdf).

## 4. Continuation

Start with a three-column physical concordance for 4Q530 2 ii + 6 + 7 i + 8–11 and 6Q8 2, Puech DJD XXXI, Stuckenbruck 2016 pp. 129–142, and Marro pp. 243–299. Mark direct join, parallel wording, thematic alignment and supplied text independently. Then compare the Gilgamesh letters in 4Q531 22d:12 against IAA/SQE and test proposed readings of the 4Q530 passage. Extend the photo/translation log to the less legible scraps, particularly disputed 1Q24, 4Q206a and 4Q533. For later transmission, work through Morano and Wilkens against the Turfan images, starting with M 648 and TM III 23–24. The printed fragment index above makes all assigned groups retrievable while their underlying preservation remains documented as partial.
