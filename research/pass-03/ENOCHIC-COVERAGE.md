# Pass 3 — Further Enochic and primeval writings

**Survey dated 2026-11-10.** Chapter ranges describe the later Ethiopic collection. Dates are working composition ranges for the works or sections specified. Qumran and other physical copy dates must be recorded separately. Selected passage readings use the OCP Rylands-based presentation (SRC-024). Inventories use Drawnel (SRC-034), Machiela (SRC-021), Elledge (SRC-035), Peters (SRC-047) and the Hermeneia volume 2 preview (SRC-048). Find the Watchers passage and witness control in [its dossier](BOOK-OF-WATCHERS.md).

| Component | Date and witness status | Relevant passages and next verification |

| Astronomical Book / Heavenly Luminaries, 72–82 | Early tradition often put in the third century BCE; 4Q208 is palaeographically late third/early second c. BCE. 4Q208–211 are Aramaic astronomical scrolls and do not reproduce the later Geʿez text word for word. Some assigned Greek P.Oxy. 2069 scraps have probable astronomical parallels. | 72:1 introduces Uriel's instruction about the luminaries and their ordered movements. 72 and 76 describe gates and courses for luminaries/winds, 80 has disturbances to their order, 81–82 address heavenly tablets, writing and transmission to Methuselah. Machiela pp. 46–58 distinguishes the more extensive Aramaic synchronistic material. Start with 4Q209 frg. 23 and 4Q210 frg. 1, near 76:13–77:4, then Drawnel's separate Astronomical Book edition and Ratzon's reconstructions of 4Q208. |
| Dream Visions, 83–90 | The Animal Apocalypse's historical allegory is often dated in the 160s BCE, around the Maccabean crisis. The date of the first dream requires separate argument. 4Q204–207 attest parts of the Animal Apocalypse in Aramaic; this does not establish a surviving Qumran copy of every dream. Drawnel also lists Greek P.Oxy. 2069 fragments assigned to parts of 85–87. | 83–84 narrate an early disaster dream and plea, 85–89 use animals and stars in an allegory of primeval history, angelic descent, violence and the Flood. Check 4Q207 at 86:1–3, then 4Q206 at 88–89 and its shorter Flood sequence relative to Geʿez. Compare Stuckenbruck and Drawnel on composition and animal symbolism. |
| Epistle and Apocalypse of Weeks, principally 91–105 | Likely layered late Second Temple materials, with the Apocalypse of Weeks often placed in the early second c. BCE. Aramaic 4Q212 preserves parts of 91–94, and 4Q204 later portions of the Epistle. Greek Chester Beatty–Michigan preserves parts around 97–107, with Cave 7 identifications around 103 proposed and debated. Coptic Antinoopolitanus 93:3–8 is known through an edition of a lost manuscript. | 91–94 frames admonition to descendants and the weeks of history. The Aramaic 4Q212 order supports reading 93:1–10 with 91:11–17 (see Elledge pp. 113–114), rather than treating their separation in Geʿez as original order. 98:4 assigns the creation of sin to humans in this exhortation. Check Drawnel pp. 401ff. and Stuckenbruck's 91–108 commentary for the implications of genre and sequence. |
| Birth of Noah appendix, 106–107 | An independently assessable Second Temple narrative unit associated with the Epistle in transmission. 4Q204 attests parts, with different ranges in older catalogues and more recent placements. Greek Chester Beatty–Michigan and the Latin 106 excerpt also attest portions, while Geʿez is continuous. | Consult [Genesis Apocryphon dossier](GENESIS-APOCRYPHON.md) for family testimony. First collate 4Q204's exact 106–107 ranges in Drawnel and Latin Royal 5 E XIII 79v–80r in its edition against Geʿez and Greek. |
| Chapter 108 appendix | Separate short Enochic writing in the Ethiopic collection. No secure Aramaic counterpart identified in consulted inventories. Date uncertain here. | 108:1 presents a book written for Methuselah and later readers. Check the ending and its witnesses separately from 106–107. |
| Book of Parables / Similitudes, 37–71 | Hermeneia 2 describes a composition around the turn of the era. Earlier and later proposals have been made. No securely identified Qumran witness in the consulted inventories. Geʿez supplies the continuous text. A missing Qumran identification gives no definitive composition date, authorship or reason for absence. | 54:7–55:2 and 60, 64–69 contain waters, Noah, judgment, hidden teachings and angels. In 65:2–12 Noah himself appeals to Enoch, with the disclosure of secrets under judgment. 69:6–9 gives Gadreel weapons and links Penemue to writing, contrasting the attribution of weapons in Watchers 8 and the positive role of writing in Jubilees 4. Check chapter 60/65–69 transitions and disputed Noachic strata in Hermeneia 2. Treat 70–71 and the Son of Man language as an independent study. |

Qumran collection evidence is positive where a fragment overlaps a work. Physical evidence for bundling is a separate problem. 4Q204–206 can support collections spanning multiple Enochic compositions, 4Q208–211 attest a separate astronomical tradition, and proposed Giants material in 4Q206a has a disputed relationship to the rest of 4Q206. Milik's hypothesis about an early collection with Giants in the place of Parables is a scholarly reconstruction, independent of the later Ethiopic table of contents.

| 5 | Pseudo-Philo Biblical Antiquities 1–4; 4 Ezra 3, 6; 2 Baruch 17, 23, 54, 56 | Compare genealogies, Adam, early humanity, Flood and responsibility. Date compositions independently of complete Latin, Syriac or later manuscript transmission. |

Treat Melchizedek's remarkable birth and later ascent literature separately from Qumran Noah. |
| 7 | Syriac Cave of Treasures and dated Christian/Jewish reception | Check recension, era and direct sources for primeval history. Its documented later transmission gives a reception dossier. |

The queue is a discovery inventory. It gives passage targets and keeps the different compositions and historical communities visible.
