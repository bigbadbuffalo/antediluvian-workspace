# Pass 4 — Annotated Watchers source catalogue

Checked 2026-11-10. See the [source register](SOURCE-REGISTER.md) and the [Watchers collation](../research/pass-04/WATCHERS-WITNESS-COLLATION.md). These records distinguish published readings and manuscripts from their digital presentations.

## SRC-052 — Langlois's photographic edition of 4Q201

- **Edition and access.** Michael Langlois, *Le premier manuscrit du Livre d’Hénoch. Étude épigraphique et philologique des fragments araméens de 4Q201 à Qumrân* (Paris: Cerf, 2008). [Author's PDF](https://michaellanglois.org/medias/langlois-2008-le-premier-manuscrit-du-livre-dhenoch--etude-epigraphique-et-philologique-des-fragments-arameens-de-4q201-a-qumran-version-basse-qualite.pdf).
- **Evidence and verification.** PRIMARY EDITION / SCHOLARLY. Selected plates, line photographs, transcriptions, and apparatus examined 2026-11-10.
- **Ancient composition and witness.** Second Temple Aramaic Watchers. 4Q201 is usually dated to the early second century BCE, with estimates varying by editor. Cross-reference SRC-021 and SRC-049.
- **Passages and limits.** 4Q201 1 c ii.6–17. See pp. 175, 177, 182, 187–188, 201–209, 219–220. Photographic boundaries support the birth-line lacuna. Langlois's proposals about missing wording and roster transmission require separate attribution.

## SRC-053 — Milik's Cave 4 Enoch edition

- **Edition and access.** J. T. Milik, with Matthew Black, *The Books of Enoch: Aramaic Fragments of Qumrân Cave 4* (Oxford: Clarendon, 1976). [Digital reading copy](https://sourcelibrary.org/book/the-books-of-enoch-aramaic-fragments-of-qumran-cave-4-milik).
- **Evidence and verification.** PRIMARY EDITION / SCHOLARLY. Selected printed-page OCR checked 2026-11-10. OCR can distort letters or marks. Important bracket boundaries compared with SRC-052, SRC-054 and SRC-056. Milik's printed plates and all of his apparatus await inspection.
- **Ancient composition and witness.** Second Temple Aramaic Enochic compositions. This pass used the two early Watchers copies 4Q201 and 4Q202.
- **Passages and limits.** 4QEnᵃ 1 iii–iv, pp. 150–151, 341–342. 4QEnᵇ 1 ii–iii, pp. 164–173, 344–346. See pp. 57–58 for the separate comparison to Damascus Document II.18. Milik's fragment/column numbering differs from the later editions. His proposed sequence and supplied readings remain edition-specific.

## SRC-054 — DEAPS digital transcriptions of 4Q201 and 4Q202

- **Edition and access.** Digital Editions of Ancient Pseudepigrapha and Scrolls, The Ohio State University. [4Q201](https://deaps.osu.edu/text_objects/17049) and [4Q202](https://deaps.osu.edu/text_objects/17050).
- **Evidence and verification.** PRIMARY TEXT PRESENTATION. Selected lines checked 2026-11-10. Digital reading pane includes conjectural supplements in brackets. Distinct reference from OCP (SRC-024).
- **Ancient composition and witness.** Second Temple Aramaic Watchers in two fragmentary early copies, commonly assigned to the second century BCE.
- **Passages and limits.** 1 En. 7:2 in the two scrolls and selected surrounding lines. Use for a presentation-level check of supplied words. Refer to print editions and photographs for physical alignment.

## SRC-055 — Black's Greek Enoch edition

- **Edition and access.** Matthew Black, *Apocalypsis Henochi Graece*, Pseudepigrapha Veteris Testamenti Graece 3 (Leiden: Brill, 1970). [Online transcription identifying Black](https://barnascha.narod.ru/books/hanokhg.htm). Dindorf's *Chronographiae*, vol. I (Bonn, 1829), pp. 20–23, 42–46, cross-referenced through Bhayro SRC-023, p. 89.
- **Evidence and verification.** PRIMARY EDITION, accessed via derivative transcription. Selected readings checked 2026-11-10 and compared with SRC-024. Black and Dindorf printed apparatus pending.
- **Ancient composition and witness.** Aramaic Watchers transmitted in Greek. Akhmim is generally dated fifth/sixth century CE. Syncellus compiled his chronicle around the turn of the ninth century CE, with later copy history.
- **Passages and limits.** Akhmim 6:7–8, 7:1–5, 8:1–4, 15:8–16:1. Syncellus excerpts at 6:1–9:4 and 8:4–10:14, plus 15:8–16:1. The two excerpts' 8:4–9:4 overlap contains variant readings. The transcription cannot substitute for Black's apparatus or a codex image.

## SRC-056 — Orlov's quotation of Drawnel on 4Q202

- **Edition and access.** Andrei A. Orlov, *Divine Mysteries in the Enochic Tradition* (Berlin/Boston: De Gruyter, 2023), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1515/9783111201924). [Author-posted excerpt](https://www.academia.edu/106038430/The_Giants_Gluttony_and_Enochs_Lost_Appetite).
- **Evidence and verification.** SCHOLARLY. Section 3.10 excerpt and p. 200 n. 828 checked 2026-11-10. Drawnel's translation appears here as a quotation.
- **Ancient composition and witness.** Second Temple Aramaic Watchers, second-century BCE 4Q202. The surrounding chapter also compares the distinct *2 Enoch* tradition.
- **Passages and limits.** Drawnel SRC-049, 4Q202 frgs. 8–12, pp. 158–163, is quoted with the height entirely bracketed. This verifies the bracket status in the quoted translation. The full edition, context, Aramaic apparatus and labelled plates remain outstanding.

## SRC-057 — Chabot's Michael the Syrian edition

- **Edition and access.** J.-B. Chabot, ed. and trans., *Chronique de Michel le Syrien*, vol. I (Paris: Leroux, 1899), French pp. 7–8; vol. IV (1910), Syriac pp. 3–4. [French](https://archive.org/details/ChroniqueDeMichelLeSyrienT.1Fasc.1translation/michael_the_syrian1) and [Syriac edition](https://archive.org/details/chroniquedemiche04mich).
- **Evidence and verification.** PRIMARY EDITION / RECEPTION. French pages and the Syriac printed hand-copy reproduced in Bhayro SRC-023, p. 356, inspected 2026-11-10. The original manuscript surface was not examined.
- **Ancient composition and witness.** Medieval Syriac chronicle by Michael, a twelfth-century patriarch. Drawnel SRC-034 §2.7 cites SOAA 0250(S), dated 1598, f. 5r and 5v margins. The chronicle quotes a distinct earlier Enochic composition.
- **Passages and limits.** Book I.4 marginal quotation, compared with main I.3–4. Brock, “A Fragment of Enoch in Syriac,” JTS 19 (1968), 626–631, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/jts/XIX.2.626), is verified bibliographically and discussed through Bhayro pp. 86–89. The full Brock article and direct comparison of SOAA 0250(S) with the derivative Karshuni manuscript remain open.

## SRC-058 — IAA images of 4Q201 and 4Q202

- **Edition and access.** Israel Antiquities Authority, Leon Levy Dead Sea Scrolls Digital Library. [4Q201 modern overview B-298333](https://www.deadseascrolls.org.il/explore-the-archive/image/B-298333), [4Q202 modern overview B-298248](https://www.deadseascrolls.org.il/explore-the-archive/image/B-298248), [4Q202 PAM 42.231](https://www.deadseascrolls.org.il/explore-the-archive/image/B-281078) and [PAM 43.203](https://www.deadseascrolls.org.il/explore-the-archive/image/B-284651).
- **Evidence and verification.** MANUSCRIPT IMAGES. 4Q202 legacy plate overviews inspected 2026-11-10. Detailed photo check of 4Q201 birth lines uses the reproduced images in SRC-052.
- **Ancient composition and witness.** Second Temple Aramaic Watchers, two Qumran copies usually assigned to the second century BCE.
- **Passages and limits.** Plate-level access is confirmed. A plate contains multiple pieces. Its inventory number and fragment labels do not automatically match Milik's letters or Drawnel's fragment numbers. Exact alignment of the 4Q202 birth-line scrap is still open.
