# Workshop — Human-centered opportunities

**All proposals below are IDEA.** No new DECISION, in-world belief, named polity, magic system or technological mechanism is established. Sources constrain attribution; they do not make a proposal canon. The workshop develops generative pressures rather than an encyclopedic list of places.

## IDEA P1-01 — The city as protection, achievement and obligation

**Anchors:** Genesis 4:12–17, CLAIM-009–013; Josephus 1.62, CLAIM-036, separately labelled RECEPTION. DEC-006 invites a mature great city; Genesis does not state its mature size.

**Proposal:** Develop a Cain-associated urban civilization whose institutions genuinely protect and support many residents, while founding households convert that dependence into obligation. Residents have reasons to love their city as well as fear its rulers. Contrast voluntary settlement, negotiated membership and coerced settlement as alternatives, not one silently chosen history.

**Control/resources/limits:** Food supplied by surrounding communities, maintenance labor, defensible access, trusted arbitration and leadership succession. No specific wall, garrison, tax or food-distribution system is fixed. The city must explain how it obtains subsistence when its legendary founder's relation to the ground is troubled.

**Ordinary people:** Shelter, work, belonging and a place to raise children; debts, duties or restrictions can complicate departure. **Powerful actors:** Offer security, manipulate fear, bargain with supplying communities or compete to define legitimate protection. **Adventure:** Escort a household seeking to leave; arbitrate protection obligations; uncover who profits from a manufactured danger. A city's merits should make the choice consequential.

**Question:** Is civic membership principally family, residence, oath, patronage or a deliberate mixture? Do rulers inherit authority from a living founder? These are author choices after social-analogy research, not findings of this pass.

## IDEA P1-02 — Pastoral communities with bargaining power

**Anchors:** Abel in Genesis 4:2 and Jabal in 4:20, CLAIM-006/015. No blanket farmer/herder war attested.

**Proposal:** Mobile livestock communities can be sophisticated partners and rivals to cities. Alternatives include seasonal cooperation, tribute/protection agreements or coercive control over routes. Their expertise and family institutions should be worth exploring in their own right.

**Control/resources/limits:** Animals, grazing, water access, weather knowledge, routes and relationships. An extraordinary city cannot simply eliminate every dependency on the countryside. Exact animal species and exchange goods require Pass 3 support or explicit invention.

**Ordinary people:** Marriage, labor, movement and mutual aid are affected by access agreements. **Powerful actors:** Herd owners, city patrons and leaders bargain over access and protection. **Adventure:** Recover stolen animals; investigate a blocked route; choose between honoring an agreement and avoiding ruin after a poor season.

**Question:** Which communities are independent, affiliated or tributary? Do not derive status from tent dwelling alone.

## IDEA P1-03 — Craft households, outsiders and transmitted skill

**Anchors:** Genesis 4:20–22, CLAIM-015–018; founder terminology interpreted in SRC-003. Genealogical association is not evidence of a statutory guild monopoly.

**Proposal:** Develop several ways of transmitting skill: large craft households, apprentices accepted by oath, specialists employed by a patron, itinerant masters, or learned institutions. Different arts can follow different arrangements.

**Control/resources/limits:** Training time, access to masters, materials, maintenance and reliable quality. If rare masters live exceptionally long, that can stabilize practice or obstruct innovation. Outsiders might gain access through marriage, service, purchase or demonstrated ability; no rule is selected.

**Ordinary people:** Training can mean security, dependence, exploitation or a path out of hardship. Domestic and support labor must appear alongside celebrated masters. **Powerful actors:** Try to keep specialists, restrict emigration or sponsor competitors. **Adventure:** Protect a departing apprentice, recover a stolen procedure, or decide whether sharing a craft will save households and endanger others.

**Question:** What can be taught freely, what is secret, and why? Sorcery or supernatural instruction must not become the default explanation for every skill before Pass 2.

## IDEA P1-04 — Music, patronage and contested memory

**Anchors:** Genesis 4:21, CLAIM-016/032. No acoustic levitation is attested there.

**Proposal:** Musicians supply entertainment, worship, mourning, celebration and transmitted accounts of the past. Alternatives include attached court performers, household traditions and traveling companies. Their value is human before any later decision about supernatural sound.

**Control/resources/limits:** Patronage, audiences, instruments, repertoire, training and reputational trust. Rulers can commission praise but may struggle to control memories preserved outside their households.

**Ordinary people:** Weddings and funerals, fame and shame, livelihood and shared memory. **Powerful actors:** Shape a dynasty's story or seek an artist's legitimacy. **Adventure:** A remembered song disputes a ruler's ancestry; a performer must choose between a patron's safety and a community's truth. These are invented conflicts, not historical claims about Jubal.

**Future extension:** If effective resonance is approved later, specify a separate mechanism, trained users, material/energy needs, accuracy, range, limits and failures. Ordinary music need not acquire every such power.

## IDEA P1-05 — Metals as a network of dependencies

**Anchors:** Genesis 4:22, CLAIM-017/033. No industrial throughput, alloy recipe or steel grade provided.

**Proposal:** Distinguish the ordinary craft economy from a city's rare masterpieces. A society may excel at one material or technique while remaining dependent on human labor and modest transport. Competing options include widely distributed useful tools, restricted elite production or a patchwork by region/art.

**Control/resources/limits:** Ore, fuel, workshop labor, training, maintenance and transport. If an extraordinary material is later invented, add its own supply bottleneck, beneficiaries, costs and limits. Do not assume that a magical process also mines ore, feeds workers and maintains tools for free.

**Ordinary people:** Repair costs and access can determine a family's livelihood. Workers may bear health, time or displacement costs; specific hazards require subsequent research. **Powerful actors:** Restrict access to masters/materials or exchange protection for output. **Adventure:** Restore a disrupted supply; investigate fraudulent quality; choose whether to equip a predatory patron to protect a workshop's dependents.

**Question:** Which capabilities change everyday productivity, which primarily express prestige, and which are too rare to transform society broadly?

## IDEA P1-06 — Living ancestors and delayed succession

**Anchors:** Genesis 5, CLAIM-022–025/030. Long lifespans of named people do not establish universal longevity, fertility, vigor or immortality.

**Proposal alternatives:** (A) Long lives are widespread; (B) longevity varies strongly between people; (C) named ancestors are exceptional. These produce different societies and should remain alternatives until decided.

**Control/resources/limits:** Property, offices, accumulated skill, health and survival still matter. A long-lived witness may remember an event accurately or have reasons to lie; age does not guarantee infallibility. A founder can outlive multiple would-be successors without the genealogy fixing a universal reproduction schedule.

**Ordinary people:** Families may owe obligations to living ancestors; ambitious adults can wait generations for independence. **Powerful actors:** Accumulate power or face coalitions of descendants. **Adventure:** Living testimony contradicts an official foundation account; an elder's incapacity triggers a succession dispute; a household asks for release from an ancient obligation.

**Question:** How common is longevity, what changes with age, and is a continuous source chronology literal in-world? No demographic simulation until these inputs are chosen.

## IDEA P1-07 — Measures, accounts and knowledge custodians

**Anchors:** Josephus's weights/measures and pillars, CLAIM-035/037/038; Uruk analogy, CLAIM-039/040. None establishes a Genesis-era cuneiform office.

**Proposal:** Institutions can achieve unusual reliability in measuring, storing, remembering and coordinating. Alternatives include civic standards, household archives, learned custodians and contested competing measures. Select a writing system separately; an archive need not automatically be a reproduction of Mesopotamian institutions.

**Control/resources/limits:** Training, durable media, trusted custody, verification and recognition of a standard. Record loss, deliberate alteration, inability to read and unequal access create dependencies. Rare extraordinary measurement or memory might coexist with ordinary transport and labor.

**Ordinary people:** A correct account can protect a household; a fraudulent one can dispossess it. **Powerful actors:** Define legitimacy through evidence, control disputes or fund preservation. **Adventure:** Find the original measure; trace an altered account; decide who may access a dangerous record. Knowledge preservation need not be a prophecy of imminent flooding.

**Question:** Which techniques are human inventions, learned discoveries, gifts or forbidden teachings? Research those provenances before making a universal rule.

## IDEA P1-08 — Violence, protection and limits on patrons

**Anchors:** Genesis 4:15,23–24 and 6:11–13, CLAIM-010/019/026. Lamech's speech is not a divine legal charter.

**Proposal:** Human institutions differ in whether they restrain private vengeance, monopolize it or disguise predation as protection. A city or mobile community can have real solidarity while powerful members claim excessive immunity.

**Control/resources/limits:** Collective trust, credible protection, willingness to testify, kin obligations and capacity to enforce settlements. No specific written code or court is attested for Cain's city. Historical analogues and deliberate fantasy law will be labelled independently.

**Ordinary people:** They must decide where to seek safety and whom to trust. **Powerful actors:** Trade patronage, cultivate fear or accept costly restraints. **Adventure:** Protect a witness, broker compensation, investigate a patron's false allegation or help a community resist an abusive protector.

**Moral frame:** The setting can recognize real wrongdoing and God's goodness while making human choices hard through conflicting duties, uncertainty and dependence. No reinterpretation of angelic rebellion as justified is proposed.

## Cross-cluster dependencies and author review

Recommended eventual workshop order: longevity and historical memory; settlement/household institutions; how knowledge is transmitted; specific extraordinary capabilities. First finish source work on Enochic instruction so a convenient institution is not accidentally treated as an ancient fact.

The following require **author DECISION before canon integration**, but none blocks the next routine research pass:

1. How common long lives are and how aging works.
2. Whether occupational founders imply bloodline traditions, teaching lineages, eponyms or a chosen mixture.
3. Which Cain-associated polities and civic institutions exist, with what scale and obligations.
4. How human arts relate to supernatural teachings: overlap, corruption, acceleration, rivalry or other explicit synthesis.
5. Which particular extraordinary abilities function, with costs and limits.

No choices are treated as approved because they appear in this document. No novel named nation, creature species, school or device has been integrated into canon.
